## 1. Sigma Algebras
The $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{A}\subseteq P(X)$ of a set $X$ is such that:
- $\emptyset, X\in \mathcal{A}$ 
- $A\in\mathcal{A}\implies X\backslash A \in\mathcal{A}$
- $A_i\in\mathcal{A}$ with $i\in\mathbb{N}$ $\implies \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\in\mathcal{A}$ 

## 2. Borel Sigma Algebra
The intersection of $\sigma$-algebras on a set is also a $\sigma$-algebra.
Let $\mathcal{M}\subseteq P(X)$, then the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{M}$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{M}$:
$$
\sigma(\mathcal{M}):=\bigcap_{\mathcal{A}_i\supseteq \mathcal{M}}\mathcal{A}_i
$$
